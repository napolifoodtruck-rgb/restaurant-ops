# Weight as the base for everything (next, after the demo)

Agreed with Napoli: weight works for everything; volume only for liquids. Every quantity is worked out in
grams inside the app; the line and the bar keep their own language on screen.

1. **Products in weight from purchase.** Each MarginEdge product gets a weight for the unit it's bought in
   (a case's pack weight, a gallon of oil ≈ 7.6 lb, an "each" lemon or burrata). Missing ones are asked one
   at a time, biggest spend first, like missing prices now. Costs then run per gram.
2. **Containers by item.** A container's weight depends on what's in it (a 1/9 pan of marinara vs lettuce),
   so each item records what a full container of it weighs, measured once on the scale ("deep 1/9 of
   mozzarella = 2.1 lb"), asked the first time a container is used for an item. A shared list of container
   names and sizes (1/9, deep 1/9, 1/6, deli pint/quart, Cambros…) in Settings keeps everyone meaning the
   same pan. Fixed conversions (tsp–gal, ml/l, oz–lb, g/kg) are shown, not editable, with fl oz (volume) and
   oz (weight) kept visibly apart.
3. **The line keeps containers, with the weight beside it:** prep lists and recipes read
   "3 × 1/9 pan (about 6.3 lb)", to keep weight on their radar.
4. **Counted things stay counted** where they're uniform (eggs, dough balls, cans, bottles), each carrying a
   weight per each for cost. Garnishes (a twist, a wheel) are counted too.
5. **The bar stays in fl oz on screen** (it's all liquid). House syrups are made with sugar by the gram: a
   syrup recipe's yield is a volume, and its weight per volume comes from its own ingredients (sugar g +
   water g over the volume it makes), so "1 oz simple syrup" in a cocktail converts on its own.
6. Waste gaps, prices and (later) stock counts on a scale all compare in the same unit.

Build after the demo; test against the real recipes so no plate cost moves unless a weight was added.
